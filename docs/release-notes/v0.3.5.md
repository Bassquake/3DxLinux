## 3DxLinux 0.3.5 — CadMouse, Universal Receiver and a 3DxWare-like panel

The project is now **3DxLinux** (github.com/Bassquake/3DxLinux, previously
SpacePilot-Pro-Linux). The driver program and its commands keep their names:
`tdxd`, `tdxctl`.

This release adds the **3Dconnexion Universal Receiver** and the **CadMouse
Compact Wireless**, from pairing to speed, scrolling and buttons. It also
brings the settings panel much closer to 3DxWare's: a tray icon, a no-device
page, the More menu, and a panel that follows the application you use. And it
fixes the SpacePilot Pro's DOM and PAN ZOOM keys, which 0.3.4 swapped by
mistake.

Everything was tested on Kubuntu 26.04 LTS (Plasma, Wayland) with a SpacePilot
Pro, a Universal Receiver and a CadMouse Compact Wireless.

### New

**Universal Receiver and CadMouse Compact Wireless.** 3Dconnexion Settings
shows the receiver and what is paired to it, as 3DxWare does:

- **The device chooser:** with more than one device, the device's name becomes
  a button that lists them with their photos.
- **The CadMouse page:** how it is connected and its battery level. A paired
  device shows up once it has been used: the receiver doesn't say whether a
  device is switched on.
- **Speed, Polling Rate and Smart Scrolling** are sent to the mouse itself,
  just as 3DxWare sends them. In `/etc/3dxlinux.conf` they are `mouse_dpi`,
  `mouse_polling_rate` and `mouse_smart_scrolling`.
- **Buttons:** the radial-menu, wheel, middle, forward and back buttons can each
  be a mouse function (done by the mouse), a keystroke or a driver function
  (sent by 3DxLinux). As in 3DxWare, click a button's text and press the keys
  ("Type a shortcut…", shown as e.g. SHIFT+C; Escape cancels), or click its
  arrow to open 3DxWare's picker:
  - a search box and its sections: 3Dconnexion, Applications, Keyboard (with
    System Shortcuts), Macros, Mouse and Radial Menus;
  - a grip at the bottom to make it taller.

  What works on Linux:
  - the mouse functions and keys;
  - the System Shortcuts, as KDE Plasma's shortcuts;
  - volume, microphone and media keys;
  - speed, Pan Zoom, Rotation and Dominant on/off, Calibrate, the settings,
    and Disabled.

  The rest (applications, macros, other radial menus, speed presets,
  double-clicks, lock keys …) is shown greyed: "Not on Linux yet".
  Per-application buttons use the **Application Specific Settings** switch.
  **Swap Middle / Right Mouse Buttons** works.
- **Common Tools radial menu:** the radial-menu button opens 3DxWare's Common
  Tools menu at the pointer (the new `tdx-radial`): Undo, Redo and
  Properties… (3Dconnexion Settings) and the Virtual NumPad. Click a slice;
  the ×, Escape, a right click or a click outside closes it, and so does
  pressing the button again. Any CadMouse or 3D mouse button can open it
  (`action_V2DK_FORWARD = commontools`).
- **Radial menus of your own:** **New Radial Menu** in the picker opens
  3DxWare's Radial Menu Editor: a name, 4 or 8 sections, each set like a
  CadMouse button (type a shortcut, or pick a keystroke or 3Dconnexion
  function; mouse functions not yet). Save gives the menu to the button, and
  the picker lists it under Radial Menus, with 3DxWare's pencil (edit) and ×
  (delete; its buttons go back to their own function) on hover. The menu's
  icon: click it in the editor for 3DxWare's "Select your icon", with an Icon
  gallery (your desktop's icon theme; 3DxWare's are Windows font glyphs) or a
  Custom icon (drop or choose an image file). `tdxctl` has `keys`, `action` and
  `radial` for scripts.
- **3DxLinux's own folder names:** the paths named after tdxd are now 3dxlinux:
  `/etc/3dxlinux.conf`, `/etc/3dxlinux.d`, `/run/3dxlinux.sock`,
  `~/.config/3dxlinux/3dxlinux.conf` (and its profiles), `~/.local/share/3dxlinux`,
  `/usr/share/3dxlinux` and `/usr/lib/3dxlinux` (the programs keep their names).
  Settings under the old names are not read: set them again (and re-import
  the 3DxWare look).
- **No password for your settings:** like 3DxWare's, your settings are now
  your own: the panel saves them in `~/.config/3dxlinux` (over `/etc/3dxlinux.conf`,
  which stays the system-wide default) and never asks for a password. tdxd reads
  them for the user at the screen, only as plain files of theirs, and ignores
  the options that are paths or its own plumbing there. Changing a profile from
  `/etc/3dxlinux.d` saves your own copy; Reset all settings removes your files.
- **Picker fix:** Redo and Save As (Ctrl+Shift+…) were rejected by tdxd
  ("unknown key KEY_SHIFT"); a bare SHIFT, CTRL or ALT now means the left one.
- **On-Screen Display:** **Always show On-Screen Display** in the CadMouse's
  Buttons window now works: 3DxWare's small dock (the new `tdx-osd`) with the
  application's icon and the middle, back and forward buttons' functions,
  following your settings and the application's profile. Drag it anywhere (the
  place is kept); a click opens the Buttons window. (3DxWare also lights a
  button while pressed; the CadMouse does its mouse functions itself, so not
  here.)
- **The tray's Quit closes everything on the screen:** the LCD Manager, the
  On-Screen Display, the Virtual NumPad, a radial menu and 3Dconnexion
  Settings; opening Settings brings them back (the dock if it is ticked). tdxd
  keeps running, so the mouse still works.
- **Virtual NumPad:** 3DxWare's number pad on the screen (the new
  `tdx-numpad`), from the radial menu or any button (`numpad`). Its keys go to
  the window with the text cursor; keypad digits work with Num Lock off too.
  Move it by its title, resize it with the dots, and set 3DxWare's options with
  the gear: next to the cursor (on Linux: where the radial menu opened), move
  the pointer onto it (Wayland: it opens under the pointer) and auto hide.
- **Acceleration and wheel lines on KDE Plasma:** "Enable acceleration" and
  "Lines" in the CadMouse's Advanced Settings change the CadMouse's own
  settings in KDE, as System Settings > Mouse does. On other desktops they are
  greyed out. "One page / one screen" is Windows only.
- **Add device** opens 3DxWare's pairing window (Device Selection:
  SpaceMouse Pro Wireless, SpaceMouse Wireless, CadMouse) and pairs a device
  with the receiver: switch it off and on while the window waits. Importing
  the 3DxWare look now also brings in the pairing program's pictures. The receiver's **Advanced Settings** lists the
  paired devices with their serial numbers. The ✕ that appears over a device
  removes it.
- From a terminal: `tdxctl pair` (`spacemouse`, `spacemousepro`),
  `tdxctl pair-stop` and `tdxctl unpair SLOT`.
  `tdxctl status` shows the receiver, what is paired, and the battery level.

**A tray icon.** The 3Dconnexion icon sits in the system tray, as in Windows.
Left click opens the settings. Right click gives you:

- **Open 3Dconnexion Settings**;
- **Write Log File**, a report on the Desktop for bug reports;
- **Start with the Desktop**;
- **Quit**, which closes the tray icon and the LCD Manager. The driver keeps
  running. Opening the settings brings the tray icon back.

**3Dconnexion Settings looks and works more like 3DxWare:**

- The app is now called "3Dconnexion Settings". The LCD Manager opens from it
  and no longer has its own menu entry.
- With no device connected, it shows 3DxWare's "No device found" page. It
  notices a device being plugged in or out while it is open.
- When a device is unplugged, every other window closes, so the main window
  shows what is left.
- **More** opens 3DxWare's menu:
  - **Import settings** and **Export settings**, as a zip of `/etc/3dxlinux.conf`
    and the profiles;
  - **Reset all settings**;
  - **Support**;
  - **About 3DxWare**.
- It follows the application you used last: no need to close and reopen it to
  see another application's buttons.
- Only one copy opens (the LCD Manager too). Starting it again brings the open
  one forward.

**FreeCAD's keys follow the workbench,** like 3DxWare's: the keys and their LCD
names change with the active workbench. This needs a small FreeCAD add-on
(`tdx-freecad-addon`), which also works with FreeCAD from Flathub. The key
sets come from your own 3DxWare installer, when you import the 3DxWare look.

**3DxWare's per-application key settings.** Importing the 3DxWare look also
turns its key settings for applications into profiles: 66 applications with
3DxWare 10.9.14. They contain keyboard shortcuts, key sequences, LCD names and
driver functions.

**Keys 1–12 and MENU do something by default.** Keys 1–10 send 3DxWare's
defaults: New, Open, Save, Copy, Cut, Paste, window to the left or right half,
switch application, Undo. On the SpaceMouse Enterprise, 11 is Redo and 12 Lock
Screen. MENU opens 3Dconnexion Settings.

### Fixed

- **DOM and PAN ZOOM** on the SpacePilot Pro are back the right way round. 0.3.4
  swapped them by mistake. The real fault was the LCD's Function Keys header,
  which showed the two icons swapped; that is fixed too.
- **The CadMouse couldn't move the cursor** with the receiver plugged in. The
  udev rule meant to stop a 3D mouse's cap moving the cursor hid every
  3Dconnexion device. It now leaves CadMice, keyboards and receivers alone.
- **Upgrading could stop at a question.** `/etc/3dxlinux.conf` and the FreeCAD
  profile were package config files. After the settings panel changed them,
  an upgrade asked whether to keep or replace them, which fails without a
  terminal. Reinstalling after removal also left them missing. The defaults
  now ship in `/usr/share/3dxlinux/defaults` and are copied in only when missing.
- **Two sets of window buttons on Wayland.** The settings windows have one set
  again, 3DxWare's own.
- **Restore Defaults on an application profile** now puts back the keys that
  profile came with, instead of none. That had stopped FreeCAD's view keys
  working.
- **The LCD showed "python3.14"** with the settings panel focused. 3DxLinux now
  ignores focus on its own windows, and names a script by the script rather
  than its interpreter.
- **No icon for Flatpak applications** (FreeCAD from Flathub) in Function Keys'
  header.

### Upgrading

- **From a package:** install the 0.3.5 one, then restart your 3D applications
  (FreeCAD, Blender, …): installing restarts the driver, and they don't
  reconnect by themselves.
- **For the CadMouse:** after installing, unplug the Universal Receiver and
  plug it in again, so the new udev rule applies.
- **The package files are now named `3DxLinux.<version>`.** Software centres
  such as Discover show the name "3DxLinux". The package itself is still
  `tdxd`, so upgrading from 0.3.4 works as before.
- **From `install.sh`:** run the new `sudo ./install.sh`.

### Hardware status

Tested on Kubuntu 26.04 LTS (KDE Plasma, Wayland), with a SpacePilot Pro, a
Universal Receiver (firmware 1.06) and a CadMouse Compact Wireless:

- the device chooser and battery level;
- Speed, Smart Scrolling, the buttons with their picker, and per-application
  buttons;
- acceleration and wheel lines on KDE;
- pairing with Add device (Device Selection);
- the tray icon;
- the settings panel's new pages;
- FreeCAD's workbench keys, with Flatpak FreeCAD;
- the DOM / PAN ZOOM fix.

Removing a device with the ✕ in the receiver's Advanced Settings was tested on
Ubuntu 22.04 (WSL). Pairing a SpaceMouse Wireless or SpaceMouse Pro Wireless
with the Universal Receiver, other CadMouse models, GNOME, the `.rpm` and the
arm64 packages are untested.
